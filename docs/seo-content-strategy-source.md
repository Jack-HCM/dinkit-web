I’ve now gone through the site, its feature pages, the current indexed footprint I can find, and the competitive SERPs around golf tracking, strokes gained, stats and performance analytics.  
My overall view is **positive**: I think Dink’it has a realistic route to organic traffic, particularly in the UK. But I would change the SEO strategy fairly substantially from simply trying to rank the homepage for “golf app”.  
The opportunity is to build Dink’it into **the UK golf performance / shot-tracking resource**, with the app sitting at the centre of it.  
  
## 1. The short version  
If this were my site, I'd build the SEO strategy around this hierarchy:  
**Golf shot tracking** → **Golf statistics** → **Strokes gained** → **Golf performance analysis** → **Shot dispersion / club distances** → **Improving your handicap** → **UK golf courses**  
And I'd use the content to funnel people towards:  
**“Track your shots → understand where you're losing strokes → know what to practise.”**  
That's a much stronger proposition than:  
“Dink'it is a golf app with AI.”  
The AI is useful, but it's not currently the thing I'd try to acquire search traffic for.  
The competitive landscape confirms this. Arccos, Hole19, Golfshot, Shot Scope and newer products such as Pinpoint and golfity are all aggressively talking about shot tracking, strokes gained, dispersion and performance analysis.  
**Dink'it's opportunity is to build topical authority around the entire problem rather than trying to outrank those companies for “golf app”.**

That is the connective tissue between all these keywords.
